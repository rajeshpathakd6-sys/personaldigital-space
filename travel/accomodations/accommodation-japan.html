Northern Japan — Tōhoku & Kantō

Days 1–5  ·  Narita → Aomori → Sendai → Nikko
City Capsule-Style ★ Great Value Days 3 & 4

Smart City Hotel

KOKO Hotel West

📍 Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture

Sendai was our surprise of the northern leg. KOKO Hotel West is a compact, efficiently designed modern hotel that strips away everything unnecessary — and the result is a genuinely pleasant stay. Rooms are small but thoughtfully done; everything works, nothing is frustrating. It sits well for exploring the Jozenji-dori tree-lined boulevards and the local gyutan (beef tongue) restaurants.

Smart, minimal rooms — no fuss, no clutter Great location for city walks and food streets Stayed two nights — a rarity for us on this trip
💰 Budget-friendly 🛌 2 nights
★★★★☆ 7.8/10

Also Stayed — Northern Leg

Day 1 Hotel Nikko Narita 📍 Narita, Chiba Prefecture Classic airport transit hotel — exactly what it needs to be. Shuttle to Terminal 1 & 2, clean rooms, and a hot meal after a long flight. Nothing remarkable, nothing disappointing.
Day 2 Super Hotel Aomori 📍 Aomori City, Aomori Prefecture We were in Aomori for the nebuta matsuri (lantern festival) atmosphere — the hotel is a reliable, no-surprises choice. Walking distance to the Auga market and the waterfront. The free natural hot spring bath in the basement was a genuine bonus at this price point.
Day 5 Annex Turtle Hotori-An 📍 Nikko, Tochigi Prefecture The most atmospheric stay of the northern leg — a small guesthouse-style property right next to Daiya River. Tatami rooms, yukata robes, and an in-house onsen. The owners are warm and the breakfast was a highlight. Essential ryokan experience without the luxury price tag.

Tokyo — The Return Base

Days 6 & 13  ·  Two stays, same hotel
Heritage Hotel ★ Loved It Days 6 & 13

Classic Tokyo Hotel

Hotel Ryumeikan Tokyo

📍 Chiyoda City, Tokyo (near Tokyo Station)

We stayed here twice — once mid-trip and again at the end — which is perhaps the clearest endorsement we can give. Ryumeikan is a lovely mid-century hotel with proper character: not a design hotel trying too hard, not a chain hotel with no soul. The location just minutes from Tokyo Station makes it an ideal operational base. Staff are genuinely attentive in the Japanese sense — present without being intrusive. Rooms are compact in the Tokyo way, but everything is quality.

5 minutes from Tokyo Station — unbeatable access Traditional hospitality with modern comfort Excellent breakfast — Japanese and Western options Stayed twice — a real endorsement
💰 Mid-range 🛌 1 night each stay
★★★★★ 9.0/10

Tokyo Booking Tips

Before You Book Tokyo

Book 3–6 months ahead — Tokyo fills fast, especially for good-value mid-range hotels Near a JR line hub (Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya) saves hours per day Don't over-size: tiny rooms are fine for a city you're rarely in Ryumeikan is often slightly cheaper booked directly Request upper floors for street-noise reduction

Japanese Alps — Matsumoto, Kamikochi & Takayama

Days 7–9  ·  The mountain heart of the trip
Mountain Onsen ★ Trip Highlight Day 8

Alpine Onsen Hotel

Kamikochi Onsen Hotel

📍 Kamikochi, Nagano Prefecture (car-free valley)

If there is one stay from our entire Japan trip we'd return to without hesitation, it's this one. Kamikochi is a car-free alpine valley — you arrive by bus only — which gives it an almost otherworldly quiet. The hotel sits right in the valley, and waking up to mist over the Azusa River with the Northern Alps as your backdrop is the kind of morning you don't forget. The indoor and outdoor onsen are exceptional. Kaiseki-style dinner included, and genuinely excellent. Book months ahead — this fills up.

Car-free valley — complete silence and mountain air Both indoor and outdoor onsen with Alps views Multi-course kaiseki dinner included Walking distance to Kappabashi and Taisho Pond Dawn mist over the river — genuinely unmissable
💰 Mid-range to Splurge 🛌 1–2 nights ideal
★★★★★ 9.5/10

Kanazawa & Kyoto — The Cultural West

Days 10–12  ·  Kanazawa → Kyoto
Traditional Inn ★ Loved It Day 10

Japanese Inn (Onyado)

Onyado Nono Kanazawa

📍 Kanazawa, Ishikawa Prefecture

Kanazawa is often called Japan's secret cultural gem — it escaped WWII bombing and retains remarkable samurai districts, tea houses, and Kenroku-en, one of Japan's great gardens. Onyado Nono is a perfect complement: a traditional-style inn at an accessible price, with communal onsen baths and that distinctive quality of Japanese quietude. You feel like a genuine resident rather than a tourist.

Traditional inn atmosphere — tatami, yukata, quiet Communal onsen — properly relaxing 10 minutes walk to Kenroku-en and Higashi Chaya Best value traditional stay of the trip
💰 Budget to Mid-range 🛌 1–2 nights ideal
★★★★★ 9.0/10

Days 11 & 12 · Kyoto

Inn Kawashima

📍 Kyoto

A reliable, well-located Kyoto inn that served us for two nights — our Kyoto base for Arashiyama, Fushimi Inari, and the Nishiki Market. Inn Kawashima has the unpretentious quality of a family-run guesthouse: clean, friendly, and in a genuinely useful part of the city. Don't expect luxury — expect honesty and good value.

💰 Budget-friendly 🛌 2 nights
★★★★☆ 7.9/10

Kyoto Booking Tips

Book Kyoto hotels 6+ months out — especially autumn Central or near Kawaramachi gives the best walking access Nishiki Market: go early — tourist crowds arrive by 10am

What We Learned

14 nights.
Everything we'd tell you.

We didn't always get the accommodation right on this trip. Some nights were brilliant; a couple were just functional. Here's the distilled wisdom — what actually mattered across 14 nights, 10 cities, and 11 different stays.

01

Book Kamikochi months in advance

It's car-free, limited capacity, and very popular. We almost missed out. If you're doing an Alps loop, lock this in first.

02

The JR Pass is your accommodation too

Staying near JR Shinkansen stops changes your daily rhythm entirely. We built our whole hotel selection around this.

03

One ryokan stay is non-negotiable

We had Annex Turtle in Nikko and Kamikochi Onsen. Both were the most memorable nights of the trip. It's not just accommodation — it's the experience.

04

Room size doesn't matter in Japan

You spend very little time in the room. What matters is location and the bath. Don't pay extra for square footage.
