Travel and Reflection

More Than
a Travel Feed

It's hard to imagine planning a trip today without the internet.

Before we've even chosen a destination, we're already watching videos, saving photographs, reading blogs, scrolling through recommendations, and building mental lists of places we'd like to visit someday. We've done it too. A beautiful photograph leads to an article. The article leads to a map. The map leads to another destination entirely. Before long, a simple idea has turned into an evening spent dreaming about a future journey.

There is something wonderful about that.

But somewhere along the way, we realised there's also a subtle trap. It's very easy to spend so much time thinking about how a place will look that you forget to think about how it will feel.

Travel can quietly become performance. Not because anyone intends for it to happen, but because we're constantly surrounded by perfectly edited images and carefully curated highlights. The temptation is to arrive somewhere already thinking about the photograph instead of the experience. We've caught ourselves doing that occasionally. And whenever we do, travel becomes a little less enjoyable.

That's probably why we've become increasingly protective of certain moments. Some of our most meaningful travel memories don't exist anywhere online. They weren't photographed, posted, shared, or turned into content. They were simply experienced.

"A conversation over breakfast. An unplanned stop during a road trip. Sitting somewhere without feeling the need to document it. Watching a sunset without reaching for a camera."

The older we get, the more we appreciate those moments. That's not to say we don't enjoy photography or sharing stories. If we didn't, Routes & Reflections wouldn't exist. But we've learned that sharing works best when it comes after the experience, not during it.

We don't want to choose destinations because they're popular online. We don't want to spend a day trying to recreate somebody else's photograph. And we definitely don't want an algorithm deciding which moments are worth remembering.

Instead, we want to share the things that genuinely caught our attention. The road that surprised us. The small café we stumbled upon. The landscape that made us stop the car. The photograph that may never be our most viewed but remains one of our favourites.

Because the goal has never been to build a travel feed.

The goal has always been to collect meaningful experiences and preserve the stories that come from them.

If those stories inspire someone else to travel, that's wonderful. But first and foremost, they need to remind us why we travelled in the first place.
